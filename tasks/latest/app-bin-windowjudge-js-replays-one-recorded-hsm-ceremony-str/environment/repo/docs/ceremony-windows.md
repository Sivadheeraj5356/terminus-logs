The input object has `until`, `events`, and exactly one top-level configuration array selecting the domain: `keys`, `packages`, `sensors`, or `players`. All timestamps are integer ticks. The program emits `{"intervals":[...]}` only. Intervals are half-open `[start, end)` ranges with `start`, `end`, `reason`, and the actor field for the active domain. Open intervals use the same `end` field with value equal to `until`.

## Confirmation boundaries

A pending transition fires at its due time, not at the event that armed it. Before each event is processed, every boundary due at or before that event's timestamp fires first, at the due timestamp itself, even when that timestamp falls between two listed events or equals the event's timestamp. After the last event, boundaries due at or before `until` still fire. An interval's `start` is the boundary fire time, not the time evidence began: alarm evidence at tick 1 with a 4-tick window opens at 5. A boundary that fires exactly at `until` while opening an interval yields a zero-length interval `[until, until)`, and that interval is emitted.

Evidence is re-evaluated only when an event for that actor is processed, and at a suspension window's end. A transition that fires at its computed boundary does not re-evaluate evidence at the fire time: when the opposite quorum already holds as a transition fires between events, the opposite boundary is armed by the next event for that actor, measured from that event's timestamp. Worked example: reporters r0 and r1, compromiseQuorum 1, clearQuorum 1, confirmCompromise 3, confirmClear 2, `until` 14. A report from r0 marking the key compromised at 2 arms the compromise boundary at 5 and the key is revoked at 5. Because r1 has never reported it counts clear, so the clear quorum already holds at 5, yet no clear boundary is armed at 5 + 2 = 7. The clear report at 9 arms it at 9 + 2 = 11, and the emitted interval is `[5, 11)`, not `[5, 7)`.

## Suspension windows

Quarantine, freeze, hold, and pause windows suspend adjudication for the affected actor. While a window is active no boundary fires for that actor, and incoming events still update recorded evidence but arm, cancel, and fire nothing. What happens when the window ends differs by domain:

- Keys and packages: a boundary that was already armed before the window began is pushed later by the suspended duration, and then the engine re-evaluates the evidence as of the window end. A pending transition whose quorum no longer holds disappears. A quorum that was reached during the window arms a fresh boundary measured from the window end, not from the mid-window event. Worked example: reporters r0 and r1, compromiseQuorum 2, confirmCompromise 5, a compromised report from r0 at 2, quarantine from 5 to 10, a compromised report from r1 at 7. Nothing is armed at 7 because the key is quarantined; the re-evaluation at 10 sees the quorum and arms the boundary at 10 + 5 = 15, not at 12.
- Sensors: the shift rule does NOT apply. At `hold_end` the deadline is rescheduled to `hold_end` plus the full `enterDwell` or `exitDwell` implied by the latest evidence, or cleared if the evidence no longer implies a transition. Worked example: high reading at 1 with enterDwell 4 arms the boundary at 5, hold from 2 to 9, and the alarm opens at 9 + 4 = 13, not at the shifted 5 + 7 = 12.
- Players: pause and resume are described in the player section.

## Partition-key compromise windows

Each key has `id`, `reporters`, `compromiseQuorum`, `clearQuorum`, `confirmCompromise`, and `confirmClear`. A `report` event carries fields `reporter` and `compromised` and replaces only that reporter's last state; reporters that have never reported count as clear. An unrevoked key arms a compromise boundary after `confirmCompromise` once at least `compromiseQuorum` reporters indicate compromise, and a revoked key arms a clear boundary after `confirmClear` once at least `clearQuorum` reporters indicate clear. If the quorum stops holding before the boundary, the pending transition disappears. `quarantine_start` and `quarantine_end` follow the suspension rules above. A `replace` event closes an open compromise interval at the replacement time and creates a new key while copying the old key's quorums and confirmation durations. Its JSON shape is flat: `{"at":6,"op":"replace","key":"hsm-key-9","newKey":"hsm-key-10","reporters":["x1","x2"]}`. The `newKey` value is the new key id string; `reporters` is a sibling field, not an object nested under `newKey`. Replace does not cancel a boundary already armed for the old key and does not clear its reporter states; the old key stays live and can open or close further intervals afterwards. Worked example: key `old` has reporter r0, `compromiseQuorum` 1, `clearQuorum` 1, `confirmCompromise` 3, and `confirmClear` 2. Reporter r0 marks `old` compromised at 1, so the first compromise opens at 4. A replace at 5 closes `[4, 5)` and creates the new key, but `old` is still present with r0's compromised report. If r0 reports compromised for `old` again at 7, the old key arms another compromise boundary at 10, and with `until` 16 it emits `[10, 16)` too. Key intervals use actor field `key` and reason `confirmed_compromise`.

## Ceremony-script admission windows

Each package has `name`, `readyQuorum`, `quiet`, and `cooldown`. An `artifact` event carries fields `dep` and `ready` and replaces only that dependency's last state; only dependencies whose latest artifact was ready count toward the quorum. An inactive package arms an admission boundary `quiet` ticks after the ready quorum is met; if the quorum drops before the boundary, the pending admission disappears. `freeze_start` and `freeze_end` follow the suspension rules above. An `invalidate` event takes effect even during a freeze: it closes an active admission interval at the event time, and when the ready quorum holds it arms the next admission boundary at the event time plus `cooldown`, re-anchoring any boundary that was already pending. A boundary armed inside a freeze window is still pushed by the full frozen duration at `freeze_end`. Package intervals use actor field `package` and reason `cache_admitted`.

## Tamper-level hysteresis windows

Each sensor has `name`, `low`, `high`, `enterDwell`, and `exitDwell`. A `reading` event carries field `value`. A value at or above `high` sets alarm evidence, a value at or below `low` sets clear evidence, and a value strictly between the thresholds leaves the evidence and any pending boundary untouched. Every alarm or clear reading re-arms the pending boundary from that reading's timestamp, including a reading that repeats the current evidence: alarm readings at 1 and 3 with enterDwell 4 fire at 7, not 5. Alarm evidence for a sensor that is not alarming arms an entry boundary after `enterDwell`; clear evidence for an alarming sensor arms an exit boundary after `exitDwell`; evidence that matches the sensor's current state clears any pending transition. `hold_start` and `hold_end` follow the sensor reschedule rule above. Sensor intervals use actor field `sensor` and reason `hysteresis_alarm`.

## Officer-clock flag-fall windows

Each player has `name`, `main`, `grace`, and `recoverOnMove`. A `turn` event names the player whose clock starts: it records the turn start, arms that player's loss boundary at turn start plus remaining main time plus `grace`, and makes that player current. Boundaries of other players stay armed, so several players can be pending at once. A `move` by the current player charges move time minus turn start against remaining main time (floored at zero), stops the clock, clears any armed boundary, and clears current; this applies whether or not the loss boundary has already fired, so the lost span is charged too. A move by a player whose loss has been confirmed also closes the open loss interval at the move time when that player's `recoverOnMove` is true; a move by a non-current player has no other effect. When a loss boundary fires, the interval opens at the boundary time and the pending deadline is consumed. Worked example: main 4, grace 1. A turn at 36 arms the boundary at 41 and the loss fires there; a recovery move at 45 closes `[41, 45)` and also charges 45 - 36 = 9 against the remaining 4, flooring it at 0, so a later turn at 49 arms the next boundary at 49 + 0 + 1 = 50.

Only a move charges a clock. A `turn` naming a new player does not stop, charge, or cancel the previously running player's clock; that player's boundary stays armed where it was. A repeated `turn` by the player who is already running re-anchors that player's boundary at the new turn start plus the unchanged remaining main time plus `grace`; the abandoned segment costs nothing. Worked example: main 3, grace 1. Turns by player a at 7, 8, and 9 leave a single boundary at 9 + 3 + 1 = 13 and the loss interval opens at 13; the segments started at 7 and 8 are never charged. If a turn by player b arrives at 10, a's boundary at 13 still fires. The fire-before-event rule from the confirmation boundaries section applies to turn events too: if a's next turn arrives exactly at 13, the boundary due at 13 fires first and the interval opens at 13; the turn is processed afterwards and does not push the boundary to 17.

`pause` and `resume` affect the whole domain. While paused no loss boundary fires. A `turn` during a pause arms its boundary normally, and at `resume` every pending boundary and every running turn start shifts by the full pause length, resume time minus pause time, even for turns taken mid-pause. A move by the current player during a pause charges move time minus turn start unshifted, paused ticks included, and clears the deadline, so the resume shifts nothing for that player. A pause that never resumes suppresses all boundary fires through `until`. Worked example: main 5, grace 1, turn at 2 arms the boundary at 8, pause at 4, resume at 10 shifts it to 14; with no further events and `until` 20 the loss interval is `[14, 20)`. Player intervals use actor field `player` and reason `flag_fall`.

A loss boundary never fires for a player whose earlier loss interval is still open: the pending deadline survives untouched until the loss is resolved. A recovery move closes only the open interval, and unless the mover is the current player it clears nothing else, so a deadline armed by a turn taken while flagged stays armed across the recovery and fires at the next evaluation, an event for any actor or the horizon, at its original due time. That due time can precede the recovery move itself, in which case the new interval opens before the move that closed the previous one and the two intervals overlap. Worked example: main 3, grace 0, recoverOnMove true. A turn at 1 arms the boundary at 4 and the loss opens at 4. A turn at 9 while still flagged arms a fresh deadline at 9 + 3 + 0 = 12, which cannot fire while the loss is open. After another player takes the turn at 13, a move at 13 closes [4, 13) and leaves the deadline armed; the evaluation before the next event fires it at 12, and a second move at 14 closes [12, 14). Both intervals are emitted even though they overlap.
