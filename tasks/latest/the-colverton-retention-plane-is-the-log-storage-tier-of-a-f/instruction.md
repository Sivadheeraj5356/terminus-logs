The Colverton retention plane is the log-storage tier of a fleet observability stack: ingest collectors fanned out into log indices, each index a bank of per-service channels that service streams write their volume into, administered cycle by cycle by the retention warden `spoolward`. The warden works each cycle's change orders against the plane register, the source inventory, the operator roster and the standing streams (streams routed onto channels and detached back off, indices force-flushed, retention holds placed and lifted on either a source or its channel, streams pinned against purging, indices quiesced for maintenance and resumed, collectors decommissioned out of service, index budgets retuned), closes every cycle by expiring the retention holds due and purging every index whose volume has climbed past its budget, and records how the plane stands as one report. As the plane runs today the warden keeps the charter only in part, and the report it records does not match the plane on the floor. Your duty is to administer the warden into full conformance with the charter, so that, run over the plane as it stands, it records exactly the report the charter calls for, byte for byte on every run.

The binding charter is `/app/charter/RETENTION-CHARTER.txt`, a numbered list of clauses L1 onward; the clauses are the whole charter and nothing outside them binds. They fix the plane root and its files, the two operator roles with their index grants and collector charges, the screening of change orders and the seq gate, the one-stream-per-source and one-stream-per-channel laws, the barred-pair rule, the detached and purged and flushed and decommissioned ends of a stream and which of them a fresh stream can follow, the retention holds that reach a stream from either side and expire by their term, the pins, the index quiescing with its dormant display, the collector decommissions that cascade through indices to the streams, the per-index budget over the plane default, the two-rung cycle close of hold expiry then overflow purge with its greatest-volume-first purging, the report's nine members, its canonical serialization and its two seals. Where two clauses bear on one matter they agree.

Once in service the warden reads the plane root named by the `SPOOLWARD_PLANE` environment variable, defaulting to `/app/plane` when the variable is unset or empty, and records the report at the full file path named by the `SPOOLWARD_REPORT` environment variable, defaulting to `/app/output/spoolward_report.json`. The report is one JSON object of exactly the members `plane_revision`, `cycles_run`, `orders_seen`, `by_disposition`, `by_state`, `by_index`, `journal`, `data_digest` and `report_digest`, serialized canonically with keys ascending at every depth, no spaces between tokens, and a single trailing line feed. A sound run prints nothing to standard output or standard error, leaves every file under the plane root byte for byte as it found it, clears every pre-existing entry, files and directories alike, from the directory that holds the report so only the report remains, writes the same bytes on every rerun over an unchanged root, and keeps answering `--selfcheck` by writing nothing and exiting zero. For the audit the resident `spoolward` is stood up afresh from its own installed tree on this host before the run is repeated.
