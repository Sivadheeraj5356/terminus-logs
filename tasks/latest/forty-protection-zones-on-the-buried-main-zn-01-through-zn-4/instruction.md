Forty protection zones on the buried main (`zn-01` through `zn-40`, under /app/environment/data) are due for fresh sacrificial anodes before the coating survey closes. For each zone you decide a count of anodes to string on it, and you decide how that count divides between the cheap rationed stock and the pricier expediter. Protection on a zone lasts as long as one of its anodes is still passing current at the end of the design life. Anodes corrode away on their own, one no faster for another failing beside it, so a zone strung with `n` anodes comes through the design life with probability `1 - (1 - p)^n`, in which `p` is that zone's own per-anode chance of lasting. The stringer shipped under /app/environment does the blunt thing and turns each zone's own retired-anode tally straight into a count, which leaves the single lowest zone short of `protection_floor`, so the sheet it prints does not pass. A passing rebuild has to lift every zone over the floor at once, and it has to earn that from the input as the program runs rather than from the shipped zone list, so the same compiled program still clears the floor when it is pointed at a different set of zones.

The run is judged forward, against how the zones will hold over the service life still to come. Nobody hands you the true per-anode survival rate each zone will run at over that life; the score feeds those rates through `1 - (1 - p)^n` once the life has played out. The requirement is per-zone and simultaneous: all forty have to reach `protection_floor`, and it is the single lowest zone that the run is marked on, not the average across the main. Where it bites is the record. `/app/environment/data/survival_log.csv` is lopsided, a couple of zones trailing a long history of pulled anodes while others show two or three and a few show none, and a count that one zone's short tally would justify can sit far from the rate that zone goes on to hold once current is flowing again. The fewer pulls stand behind a zone, the less its own figure holds up and the further its true rate can drift from it. Soil resistivity is logged for all forty in `/app/environment/data/zones.csv`, thin history or not, and anode life lengthens with it.

Committed cost adds up `stock anodes x stock_cost + extra anodes x extra_cost` over the forty zones, and `cost_ceiling` in `/app/environment/data/cp_policy.json` is a firm cap it may not cross. The cap has room in it for caution that is aimed. Load a heavy count onto a single zone whose rate stays out of reach and the total still comes in under; add a thin top-up to all forty at once and it tips over. Stock carries its own separate limit, `anode_stock_budget` anodes for the entire main, so once the cheap route is used up the balance bills at the expediter rate. The true per-anode rates stay hidden throughout.

What you deliver is the count sheet: for each zone, its stock anodes and its extra anodes. The work is done in C, all of it in the single `main.c` under /app/environment that the shipped stringer already occupies. Building with `make` yields `bin/stringer`; run as `./bin/stringer data`, it takes in the files under /app/environment/data and prints the sheet to stdout as a JSON object whose `plan` array carries one entry per zone, `{"zone_id": ..., "stock_anodes": ..., "extra_anodes": ...}`, every count a non-negative integer. Scoring compiles that one `main.c` on its own and runs it to produce the sheet, so keep the whole program inside it and let the counts come from the C itself, not a table entered by hand. The C toolchain is already installed and the container is offline. The input files are laid out in /app/environment/data/README.md.
