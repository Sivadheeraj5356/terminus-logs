# PFSEAL2 threat model

The protected assets are secret confidentiality, envelope authenticity, deployment and metadata-revision binding, key-rotation lineage, anti-replay state, and the integrity of both deployment-local and cross-deployment audit history. Image annotations, envelope bytes, plaintext fields before authentication, job identifiers, revisions, lease state, key-share metadata, prior audit heads, prior transparency checkpoints, and event-MMR frontiers must be treated as adversarial inputs. A quorum of eligible Ed25519 witness roots in `pfseal_key_witnesses` authenticates the effective three-share key set; the verifier does not claim security after compromise of two eligible witness private keys or the host running DuckDB.

The verifier must fail closed before releasing or persisting plaintext. Structural checks and the revision-scoped Ed25519 key-set quorum precede key policy and envelope authentication, the header HMAC is compared in constant time before AES-GCM is attempted, and no decrypted value becomes trusted until GCM, canonical JSON, deployment binding, validity-window, and replay checks all succeed. A historical readable key is accepted only with a valid active-key successor proof bound to the deployment and metadata revision.

Successful decisions create evidence rather than exposing the secret: a key-attestation digest, plaintext digest, authenticated-header digest, monotonic replay counter, rotation decision, deployment-specific hash-chain link, global snapshot Merkle checkpoint, and append-only event-MMR root. Rejections must not advance that evidence. Database values that cannot be represented exactly in JavaScript are fatal before a lease or claim mutation, and damaged audit heads, checkpoint anchors, or accumulator frontiers are fatal before a new proof is appended.
