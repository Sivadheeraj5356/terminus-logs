We run a small group of replicas that keep an ordered, authenticated log of client operations. Every replica shares one cluster key, but the network is hostile: it drops, reorders, duplicates, and replays messages, opens and heals partitions, and injects messages no replica ever sent. You write the replica fresh in /app/src/LogReplica.java against the Replica interface, MsgKind tags, and AuthCode helper. /app/docs/interface.md is the type, lifecycle, and timing reference, /app/docs/contract.md states the guarantees the group keeps, and bash /app/smoke.sh replays sample schedules.

Run Viewstamped Replication over the six MsgKind tags. One replica is the primary for a view; it numbers each operation, prepares it to the others, and commits it once a majority store it. When replicas stop hearing the primary they change view, collecting a majority of view-change messages that carry each replica's log, and the new primary adopts the most current one. Authenticate every message under the cluster key and act on it only in the exchange it belongs to.

A replica may stop and come back with no memory. Stamp every message with an incarnation for its current life; when a peer shows a higher incarnation, discard its earlier votes, so a stale acknowledgement cannot complete a fresh majority. A returning replica stays a non-participant until a majority of peers, answering a fresh challenge token it chose, catch it up; one peer is not enough. Once acknowledged, an operation stays present and ordered through every later view, and while a majority connect, new operations keep committing.
