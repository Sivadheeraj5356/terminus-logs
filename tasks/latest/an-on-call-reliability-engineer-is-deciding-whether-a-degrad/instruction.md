An on-call reliability engineer is deciding whether a degraded stateful service group may move to a standby host without split-brain writes, exposed held services, or a lost rollback path. The incident evidence is under `/app/records`, the authoritative operating rules are in `/app/README.md`, and the completed failover readiness dossier must live under `/app/dossier`. The dossier has exactly sixteen artifacts across `/app/dossier/decision`, `/app/dossier/evidence`, `/app/dossier/attestations`, and `/app/dossier/index`; each artifact must describe the same incident epoch, service group, selected host, evidence basis, and safety conclusion.

Place an executable operational assessment entrypoint at `/app/solve.sh`. It must read the current `/app/records` at runtime and issue the dossier from that incident evidence, because the checker may replace the estate with another same-contract failover situation before invoking it. The public rules cover incident-epoch currency, health precedence, quorum counting, lease fencing, storage mount exclusivity, dependency ordering, route exposure, credential validity, rollback blockers, exact status strings, canonical ordering, artifact integrity, and the earliest-blocker semantics for an unsafe cutover.

A passing dossier makes one globally consistent failover verdict and binds it to the evidence. Local records that look reasonable on their own are not enough: quorum attestations must agree with health, fencing with mounts, dependency order with credentials and held services, routes with the hold list, rollback with downstream barriers, and the final index with the normalized bytes of every other artifact.
