# Aurora Claims Risk — 2027Q1 Model Review Dossier

Consolidated standing instructions, review board record, and promotion controls for the 2027Q1 challenger cycle. Classification: internal, second line. Issued by the Model Risk function following the close of the quarter.


## 0. Standing, precedence, and how conflicts are resolved

This dossier consolidates the standing instructions for the 2027Q1 promotion cycle. It
supersedes the 2026Q4 pack in full. Readers should be aware that the body of this document
retains a good deal of historical narrative: proposals that were tabled and never adopted,
positions that were later reversed, and figures that were correct when first minuted and are
no longer correct today.

Where any statement in the narrative sections conflicts with a numbered clause in Part I, the
numbered clause governs. Where a numbered clause in Part I conflicts with an entry in the
amendment log at Appendix E, **the amendment log governs**. The amendment log is the only
authoritative record of the rules in force at the close of the quarter — thresholds, scoping,
and the determination rules including which models sit inside the regulated portfolio. Where the
body of Part I names a portfolio, a threshold, or a scoping rule and the amendment log records a
different position, the log is the one in force. A proposal recorded in the minutes is not an
amendment; only entries carrying an amendment number and an effective date are binding.

The effective date is not decoration. An amendment binds an observation recorded **on or after**
the instant it takes effect, which is midnight UTC at the start of its effective date, and it does
not reach backwards. An observation recorded before that instant is judged under whatever rule the
amendment replaced. The board has been firm on this: a run is not penalised under a rule that did
not exist when its features were scored, and the quarter under review straddles the effective date
of at least one amendment.

## 1. The feature-audit bundle

### 1.1 Anatomy

Each quarter the Polars feature-audit pipeline emits a bundle: a directory of members
accompanied by `manifest.json`. The manifest is the index of record. Every member carries a
`path`, a `format`, a declared `rows` count, a `status`, and — for observation members — the
units in which its numeric columns are expressed and the encoding of its timestamps.

The declared `rows` count is advisory. It is a producer-side count taken before sealing and it
is known to disagree with the delivered file in at least two historical bundles. Loaders must
not treat it as a validation gate, and must not reject a member because its row count differs
from the manifest.

### 1.2 Release status and quarantine

The `status` field is authoritative and is the only gate on ingestion. A member whose status is
`released` is ingested. A member whose status is anything other than `released` — in practice,
`quarantined` — is excluded in its entirety. Its rows are not loaded into the audit tables, are
not counted toward any aggregate, and do not participate in supersession. A quarantined member
is treated exactly as though it had never been delivered.

The producing pipeline quarantines a member when it cannot vouch for the member's provenance.
It does not remove the file, because the file is retained for forensic purposes. Several
incidents in this quarter were caused by loaders that walked the directory rather than reading
the manifest, and thereby ingested quarantined rows.

### 1.3 Units and encodings

The CSV observation members express `psi` and `null_rate` as ratios, and encode `observed_at`
as an ISO-8601 timestamp carrying an explicit UTC offset. They are RFC 4180 CSV, and the writers
behind them do not agree with one another: some emit bare fields, others quote every field they
write. The manifest records which, in the member's `quoting` entry, and a loader that splits a
quoted line on commas without honouring the quoting will carry the quote marks into the
identifiers it looks up and silently match nothing. Offsets other than zero appear in the
data and are not errors; they reflect the region in which the scoring worker ran.

The legacy TSV members are the residue of the pre-migration pipeline and use different
conventions throughout. There is more than one of them, they were written by different vintages of
that pipeline, and they do not agree on the order of their columns: each names its columns in its
header row, and the header is the only thing that says what a column holds. A loader that reads a
legacy row by column position rather than by column name will read one of these files correctly
and the other one wrongly, and the values it lands on will still parse as numbers. It expresses stability as `psi_bp` in basis points, where one basis
point is one ten-thousandth of a ratio unit; it expresses null rates as `nulls_ppm` in parts
per million; and it encodes `observed_at_ms` as milliseconds since the Unix epoch in UTC. It
also identifies cohorts by their legacy alias rather than by their registered identifier, and
it orders its columns differently from the CSV members.

All timestamps are normalised to UTC before any comparison is performed. Local wall-clock
values are never compared to one another directly.

### 1.4 Recomputed stability scores

The stability figures in the audit members are what the scoring workers emitted, and for most
observations they are the figures the review uses. A small number are not. The bundle carries a
distribution member, listed in the manifest with the `psi_bins` format, that holds the per-bin
reference and actual distributions for the observations caught by the February binning incident.
For any observation that appears in that member, the population-stability index is recomputed from
its bins and the recomputed value supersedes the raw `psi` the audit member reported; every other
observation keeps its reported figure. An observation is matched to its distribution by the same
natural key used everywhere else, run, feature and cohort. The recompute is governed by A-15 and
the method is set out in clause 7.6; a pipeline that trusts the raw column for these observations
is scoring them on the number the incident invalidated.

## 2. Reference data and referential integrity

The registry is the system of record for runs, features, and cohorts. The bundle is not.

An observation is discarded at load time, silently and without an error, when any of the
following holds:

- its `run_id` does not appear in `model_run`;
- its `feature_id` does not appear in `feature_catalog`;
- its cohort cannot be resolved against `cohort_register`.

Legacy members identify a cohort by the alias recorded in `cohort_register.legacy_alias`. An
alias with no registered owner resolves to nothing and the observation carrying it is
discarded. The audit tables never contain a row that fails referential integrity, and the
loader must not invent registry entries to accommodate the bundle.



## 3. Cohort scoping

Cohort scope is decided by the register, not by the bundle.

- A cohort whose status is `ACTIVE` is in scope.
- A cohort whose status is `PROVISIONAL` is in scope. The provisional designation constrains
  what the board may conclude from the cohort; it does not remove the cohort from the audit.
- A cohort whose status is `SUSPENDED` is out of scope for evaluation, without exception and
  regardless of when the observation was recorded. Suspended cohorts exist to carry shadow
  traffic and their observations have no governance meaning.
- A cohort whose status is `RETIRED` remains in scope for observations recorded **strictly
  before** the instant of its retirement, and is out of scope from that instant onward. The
  register records `retired_on` as a date; the retirement instant is midnight UTC at the start
  of that date.

The board considered, and rejected, a proposal to drop retired cohorts from the audit entirely.
The reasoning is recorded in the minutes: a cohort that was live for two thirds of the quarter
carries evidence about the quarter, and discarding it would flatter the models that were
scored against it.

Observations belonging to out-of-scope cohorts are still loaded and retained in the audit
table. They are excluded from evaluation, not from the record.



## 4. Supersession

The natural key of an observation is the tuple `(run_id, feature_id, cohort_id)`. A bundle may
carry more than one observation for the same natural key: the pipeline reruns cohorts, and the
legacy extract overlaps the CSV members for part of the quarter.

Exactly one observation survives for each natural key. The survivor is the observation with the
**latest** `observed_at` instant, compared in UTC. Where two observations for the same key
share the same instant, the observation drawn from a CSV member prevails over the observation
drawn from the legacy member; the legacy extract is the less trustworthy of the two and loses
every tie.

Only the surviving observation is written to the audit table. Superseded observations are
discarded on load. Supersession is decided across the whole bundle, not within a single member:
an observation in one CSV part may supersede an observation in another part, and either may
supersede a legacy row.



## 5. The run event stream

`events/run_events.jsonl` records the lifecycle of each run. Two event types carry governance
weight.

**AUDIT_ABORTED.** An abort is meaningful only when it is *terminal* — that is, when it is the
latest event recorded against the run. A run whose latest event is `AUDIT_ABORTED` is withheld:
it receives no promotion determination and no row in the promotion audit. A run that aborted and
was subsequently re-run to completion is not withheld; its latest event is the completion, and
the abort is merely history. Loaders that search for the presence of an abort anywhere in the
stream will withhold runs that recovered, and that is a defect.

**RESCORED.** A rescore means the reference window was rebuilt at the instant recorded. Scores
derived against the old window are not comparable to scores derived against the new one, and
carry no weight. Where a run has been rescored, only observations recorded **at or after** the
latest rescore instant are evaluated. Observations recorded before it remain in the audit
table, but they are not eligible and they contribute to no aggregate — not to the row count, not
to the drift count, and not to the maximum stability figure.

Where a run carries several rescores, the latest one governs; the earlier ones were themselves
superseded.



## 6. Adequacy and scoring status

Every eligible observation is classified exactly once, in the following order of precedence.

1. **Unscored.** The pipeline emitted no usable stability value: the `psi` field is empty, or it
   holds `NaN`. An unscored observation cannot raise a drift finding and never contributes to
   the maximum stability figure. It is counted, and the count matters: see clause 8.
2. **Underpowered.** The observation carries a stability value, but its `sample_count` is below
   the adequacy floor of **500**. Below means below: an observation of exactly 500 is adequate and
   is scored. An underpowered observation is never eligible to raise a drift
   finding and never contributes to the maximum stability figure, however large its stability
   value happens to be. A high stability value on a thin sample is noise, and the board has been
   badly served by it before.
3. **Scored.** Everything else. Only scored observations are tested against a drift threshold,
   and only scored observations contribute to the maximum stability figure.

The order matters. An observation with no stability value and a thin sample is unscored, not
underpowered. All three classes count toward the number of rows considered for the run.



## 7. Drift thresholds

A scored observation constitutes a drift finding when its stability value is **at or above** the
threshold applicable to the feature's family. The comparison is inclusive: a value exactly equal
to the threshold drifts.

The thresholds in force at the close of the quarter are set out in the amendment log at
Appendix E and are summarised here for convenience. The general threshold is **0.20** and it
applies to every family for which no other figure has been enacted. Several families carry an
enacted figure of their own, and in one case that figure was enacted only part-way through the
quarter; the amendment log is the authority for which families these are, what each figure is,
and from when it binds, not this paragraph.

### 7.1 Provisional cohorts

A cohort admitted to the register on a provisional basis is watched more closely than an
established one, because the book behind it is thin and a shift there is a leading indicator
rather than a lagging one. An observation recorded in a cohort whose register status is
`PROVISIONAL` therefore drifts **0.05 below** the threshold that would otherwise apply to its
family. The surcharge moves the threshold, not the observed value, and it composes with the
family figure rather than replacing it: a `claim_history` observation in a provisional cohort
drifts at 0.15, a `sensitive_attr` observation in one drifts at 0.05.

The surcharge applies to provisional cohorts only. It does not apply to active cohorts, and it
does not apply to a retired cohort during the window in which it remains in scope. It is also
dated: the surcharge was enacted part-way through this quarter, and by clause 0 it reaches only
those observations recorded on or after it took effect. An observation recorded in a provisional
cohort before that instant is tested against its plain family threshold.

The surcharge is the standing treatment for provisional cohorts, and it should be read as the
closer watch those books are held to for the whole of the quarter under review; the fairness
function's note in the 2027Q1 pack describes it as applying to every provisional observation in
the audit. Where that description and the amendment log part ways on the window it covers, the
amendment log governs — clause 0 is not suspended for this figure any more than for the others,
and its close-out carries an effective date exactly as its enactment did.

### 7.2 Counting

A run's drift count is the number of **distinct features** carrying at least one drift finding.
A feature that drifts in four cohorts drifts once. This was contested — the original proposal
counted drifting rows, which double-counted broad features and made the auto book look far worse
than it was — and the count is now taken over distinct features.

The maximum stability figure for a run is the largest stability value among its scored,
eligible observations. Where a run has no scored eligible observation, the figure is zero.


### 7.3 Historical note on the sensitive-attribute figure

The standing instruction issued in 2026Q2 set a single population-stability threshold of 0.25 for
the attributes the fairness function watches most closely, on the reasoning that those features
are coarsely banded and move a great deal for benign reasons. That figure was current when the
2026Q2 pack was issued and it is reproduced here because several models were promoted against it
and their inventory entries still refer to it. It is no longer the figure in force; Amendment A-2
replaced it, and any pipeline still applying 0.25 to that family is applying a threshold the
board withdrew two quarters ago.


### 7.4 Historical note on the macroeconomic proposal

The November sitting spent the better part of an hour on the macroeconomic overlay. Gustav
Thorsen presented an analysis showing that the unemployment and CPI series had moved far enough
to generate findings on every run in the quarter, and proposed relaxing the family threshold to
0.30. The proposal attracted support but was not put to a vote, because the fairness function
asked for time to consider whether relaxing a threshold in-quarter set a precedent. The figure of
0.30 therefore never took effect. It appears in the November minutes, in the draft circulated
before the December sitting, and in at least one model inventory entry, and it is wrong in all
three places. The figure the board eventually enacted, in January, was 0.35.


### 7.5 The telematics tightening

The telematics features — the harsh-braking rate and the night-driving ratio, which make up the
`telemetry` family — were the subject of a data-quality escalation in February. The telematics
provider changed a sampling window without notice, and the resulting instability was large enough
that a shift the general threshold would have waved through was, for these features, a genuine
signal the board wanted surfaced. The board therefore tightened the tolerance for the `telemetry`
family to **0.15**, below the general figure, rather than above it. The tightening was enacted
mid-quarter and, like every amendment, it is not retrospective: a telematics observation recorded
before it took effect is judged against the general 0.20 threshold in the ordinary way, and only
observations recorded on or after the effective date are held to the 0.15 figure. Amendment A-11
is the authority, and it carries the effective date.

### 7.6 Recomputing the stability index

The population-stability index for a bin distribution is the sum, over the bins, of the quantity
(actual share − expected share) multiplied by the natural logarithm of (actual share ÷ expected
share). The expected share of a bin is given directly in the distribution member. The actual share
of a bin is its actual count divided by the total actual count across all of that observation's
bins. Because a zero share makes the logarithm undefined, both shares are floored at **0.0005**
before the ratio is taken, so an empty bin contributes a finite term rather than dropping out or
blowing up. The index is taken over the bins exactly as provided, with no re-binning, smoothing or
normalisation beyond that floor. The result is the stability value carried into scoring for that
observation, in place of the raw figure, and it is compared against the same family thresholds as
any other stability value.

## 8. The promotion determination

Each run that is neither withheld nor devoid of eligible observations receives exactly one
determination. The clauses below are applied **in order**, and the first clause that matches
decides the run. Later clauses are not consulted.

1. **Conduct override.** If the run's model belongs to the regulated portfolio *and* the run
   carries a drift finding on a feature in the `sensitive_attr` family, the determination is
   `REJECT`. This clause is absolute; it fires regardless of how few features drifted or how
   modest the stability values were. The regulated portfolio is listed at clause 8.1.
2. **Material drift.** If three or more distinct features drifted, or the maximum stability
   figure is at or above **0.50**, the determination is `REJECT`.
3. **Manual review.** If at least one feature drifted, or the run carries at least one unscored
   observation, the determination is `HOLD`. An unscored observation on a monitored feature
   means the board cannot see the feature at all, and a model whose features cannot be seen is
   not promoted on trust.
4. **Clear.** Otherwise the determination is `PROMOTE`.

A run whose latest event is an abort receives no determination at all, and a run with no
eligible observation after scoping receives no determination either. Neither appears in the
promotion audit.

### 8.2 Systemic-feature escalation

The determinations above are made a run at a time, but the board also watches for a feature that
is drifting right across the book rather than on a single model. A feature that raises a drift
finding in three or more distinct audited runs in the cycle is treated as **systemic** for the
cycle: a drift that shows up on one run is a model problem, but the same feature drifting across
several is a data problem the board will not promote through.

Once the per-run determinations are settled, one further pass is made over them. A run whose
determination is manual review, and which carries a drift finding on a feature that is systemic
for the cycle, is escalated to a rejection. The escalation is applied only after the ordinary
determination and only ever turns a hold into a rejection: it does not soften a rejection already
reached under the conduct override or the material-drift clause, and it does not disturb a run
that cleared. The systemic count is taken over distinct runs, not over the number of drift
findings — a feature drifting three times on one run is not systemic, while the same feature
drifting once each on three runs is. Amendment A-14 carries the rule.

## 9. Deliverables

The migration produces two tables and one file.

### 9.1 `feature_audit`

Every observation that survives supersession and referential integrity is written here,
including observations that are later found ineligible — out-of-scope cohorts, unmonitored
features, and pre-rescore observations are all retained. The table is the record of what the
bundle said; eligibility is a question asked of it afterwards.

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `run_id` | `VARCHAR(64)` | |
| `feature_id` | `VARCHAR(64)` | |
| `cohort_id` | `VARCHAR(64)` | resolved identifier, never a legacy alias |
| `psi` | `DOUBLE` | nullable; null for unscored observations |
| `null_rate` | `DOUBLE` | ratio, never ppm |
| `sample_count` | `INTEGER` | |
| `observed_at` | `TIMESTAMP` | normalised to UTC |
| `source` | `VARCHAR(16)` | `csv` or `legacy` |

### 9.2 `promotion_audit`

One row per run that received a determination.

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `run_id` | `VARCHAR(64)` | |
| `model_name` | `VARCHAR(64)` | from `model_run` |
| `model_version` | `VARCHAR(32)` | from `model_run` |
| `cohorts_evaluated` | `INTEGER` | distinct cohorts among the run's eligible observations |
| `rows_considered` | `INTEGER` | eligible observations: unscored + underpowered + scored |
| `drifted_features` | `INTEGER` | distinct features carrying a drift finding |
| `underpowered_rows` | `INTEGER` | |
| `unscored_rows` | `INTEGER` | |
| `max_psi` | `DOUBLE` | largest stability value among scored eligible observations; zero if none |
| `null_rate_p95` | `DOUBLE` | 95th percentile of the eligible null rates; see below |
| `decision` | `VARCHAR(16)` | `PROMOTE`, `HOLD`, or `REJECT` |

The percentile is taken by the **nearest-rank** method, and the board is particular about this
because the interpolated variety produces a figure that appears in no observation and cannot be
traced back to a cohort. Sort the null rates of the run's eligible observations ascending — all
of them, whether the observation was scored, underpowered, or unscored, since every observation
carries a null rate — and take the value at the one-based position `ceil(0.95 x N)`. No
interpolation between neighbouring values, and no averaging. Where a run has a single eligible
observation the percentile is that observation's null rate; where it has none it is zero.

### 9.3 Migration versioning

The bundle load is enacted as version **7** of the registry schema and is recorded in
`schema_version` exactly as the six migrations before it were. Re-running the runner against an
already-migrated database applies nothing and records nothing further: the ledger carries one
row for version 7, not two. The audit tables are not rebuilt on a second pass.

# Appendices

## Appendix A. Glossary

**Adequacy floor.** The minimum sample count an observation must carry before it may raise a drift finding.

**Amendment.** A numbered change to a standing instruction, recorded in the amendment log and binding from its effective date.

**Basis point.** One ten-thousandth of a ratio unit; the unit in which the legacy extract reports population stability.

**Bundle.** The sealed set of files produced by a single run of the feature-audit pipeline.

**Champion.** The model version currently serving production traffic for a given book.

**Challenger.** A candidate model version competing for promotion against the incumbent champion.

**Cohort.** A named partition of the portfolio over which features are monitored independently.

**Drift finding.** A determination that a feature's population stability index has reached or exceeded the threshold applicable to its family.

**Eligible observation.** An observation that survives cohort scoping, feature monitoring status, and the reference-window cut-off.

**Family.** The governance grouping a feature belongs to; the family determines which drift threshold applies.

**Manifest.** The machine-readable index of a bundle's members, their formats, and their release status.

**Natural key.** The tuple that uniquely identifies an observation within the audit domain.

**Population Stability Index.** A divergence statistic comparing a feature's current distribution against its reference distribution.

**Promotion audit.** The canonical, per-run record of the promotion determination for a review cycle.

**Quarantine.** The state of a bundle member that has been withheld from ingestion by the producing pipeline.

**Reference window.** The period whose distribution a feature is scored against; rebuilding it invalidates earlier scores.

**Regulated portfolio.** The subset of models subject to heightened conduct scrutiny under the fairness policy.

**Rescore.** The act of rebuilding a reference window and re-deriving a run's scores against it.

**Sealed.** The state of a bundle once the producing pipeline has written its manifest and stopped.

**Supersession.** The replacement of an earlier observation by a later one carrying the same natural key.

**Terminal event.** The last event recorded against a run in the ordered event stream.

**Underpowered observation.** An observation whose sample count falls below the adequacy floor.

**Unscored observation.** An observation for which the pipeline emitted no usable stability value.

**Withheld run.** A run that receives no promotion determination because its audit did not complete.

## Appendix D. The canonical promotion audit export

The export is the artefact the review board signs. It is compared byte for byte against an
independently produced copy, and a checksum mismatch is treated as a control failure, so the
contract below is exact.

- The file is UTF-8, with no byte-order mark.
- Lines are terminated by a single line feed (`0x0A`). Carriage returns never appear.
- The first line is the header, written exactly as:

```
run_id,model_name,model_version,cohorts_evaluated,rows_considered,drifted_features,underpowered_rows,unscored_rows,max_psi,null_rate_p95,decision
```

- One line follows per row of `promotion_audit`.
- Rows are ordered by the **severity of the determination, most severe first**: every `REJECT`,
  then every `HOLD`, then every `PROMOTE`. The board reads the rejections before it reads anything
  else and will not accept a file that buries them. Severity is not a matter of opinion: the
  registry records it as `rank_order` in the `promotion_decision` table, where a *higher* rank is
  the more severe determination, so the file is ordered by that column **descending**. Within a
  determination, rows are ordered by `run_id` ascending, compared as byte strings.
- Integer columns are written in their plain decimal form, with no padding, no sign, and no
  grouping separator.
- `max_psi` is written with **exactly four digits after the decimal point**, rounded half up.
  A value of zero is written `0.0000`, never `0` and never `0.0`.
- `null_rate_p95` is written with **exactly six digits after the decimal point**, rounded half
  up. The same rule about zero applies: `0.000000`.
- Fields are separated by a single comma. No field is quoted and no field is escaped; no value
  in this table can contain a comma.
- The final line is terminated by a line feed. The file ends there: there is no trailing blank
  line and no unterminated final record.
