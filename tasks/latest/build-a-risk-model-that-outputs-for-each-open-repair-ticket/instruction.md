Build a risk model that outputs, for each open repair ticket, a calibrated probability it needs a repeat truck-roll within thirty days and whether to assign a senior technician now. You inherit a pipeline at /app/analysis.R whose validation looks strong but does not generalize to the open tickets; diagnose why and make them trustworthy. The data is /app/data/service_ledger.csv; schema in /app/contracts/schema.md, artifacts in /app/contracts/output_contract.md.

Each row is one ticket. repeat_visit is 1 if it needed a repeat truck-roll, 0 if resolved first time, blank for the open tickets in the newest service_month, which you must score and which sit a few months past the last labeled month. Repeat rates rise across service_month and decelerate; the flattening shows only across the full labeled history, not the recent window, and the job_complexity effect differs by equipment_class, so fit the whole history and keep the probabilities calibrated within every equipment_class across the job_complexity range. Missing a repeat costs about five times a needless senior dispatch, so pick the cost-minimizing threshold, not the half. One field settles only once the outcome is known yet carries a provisional value on open tickets, so it must not drive the model whatever its name; leaning on it scores well then collapses on the open batch. The newest month adds an equipment_class with no history, needing a fallback that scores it calibrated to its own repeat rate, not merely ranked. Fit on the genuine ticket cohort only: a few tickets appear more than once, prior_tickets_90d uses -1 for a never-captured value, dispatch_channel has occasional junk tokens, and internal qa_audit rows are not real tickets.

Write the artifacts in /app/contracts/output_contract.md into /app/outputs, keeping your solution at /app/analysis.R.
