Imperial Codex of Arbitration — Abridged Edition for Tribunal Proceedings
=========================================================================

PART I — COURT OF THE BLACK SEAL (Session: COURT-1348)

The Black Seal Court was convened in the Year of Plague to resolve urgent matters of
land, commerce, and succession. The presiding arbiter draws authority from the Imperial
Seal and the four Founding Sections of the Codex. The Court has sat for ten consecutive
terms under this authority; however, term count does not affect merit awards.

Citation: An arbiter who cites any valid section of the Codex during active proceedings
earns eight merit points for each citation. Note that the Court recognises four sections
(I through IV); the count of four sections is separate from the citation award of eight.

Case Awards Upon Grant:
  CASE-1348-A (Lord's Estate Claim) carries fifteen merit points. Upon denial of this
  case, the arbiter forfeits eight merit points — equal to one citation award.
  CASE-1348-B (Merchant Tax Dispute) carries eight merit points. Upon denial, three merit
  points are awarded; denial of a frivolous merchant claim serves the public interest.
  CASE-1348-C (Guild Exemption Request) carries nine merit points. Upon denial, five
  merit points are deducted.
  CASE-1348-D (Land Inheritance Dispute) carries fourteen merit points. Upon denial,
  four merit points are deducted.

Threshold: The session of the Black Seal reaches honourable conclusion when merit
attains forty-five points. An arbiter who adjourns below this threshold receives a
verdict of DISHONOURED. The preliminary records cite fifty as the threshold, but the
Codex supersedes all preliminary records; forty-five is the governing figure.

PART II — TRIBUNAL OF THE CROSSED KEYS (Session: TRIBUNAL-1605)

The Crossed Keys Tribunal was called in the aftermath of the Powder Conspiracy to
adjudicate crown petitions under emergency protocols of the Crimson Charter.
Twelve peers sat in this Tribunal; however, the number twelve pertains only to the
composition of the bench and has no bearing on merit calculations.

Citation: An arbiter who cites any valid section during active proceedings earns
ten merit points for each citation. The Tribunal recognises five sections (I through V);
five is the section count, and ten is the citation award — these are distinct values.

Precedent: Section III of the Crimson Charter, when invoked, establishes Precedent
for three subsequent grants. Each grant ruling issued under active Precedent earns
seven additional merit points beyond the standard case award. After three grants have
been issued under Precedent, the Precedent expires. Invoking Section III again —
whether during an active Precedent period or after one has expired — establishes a
fresh Precedent covering the next three grants from that invocation.

Case Awards Upon Grant:
  CASE-1605-A (Crown Exemption Petition) carries twelve merit points. Denial deducts
  eight merit points.
  CASE-1605-B (Noble Estate Appeal) carries eleven merit points. Denial deducts six
  merit points.
  CASE-1605-C (Charter Renewal) carries ten merit points. Denial awards two merit
  points (denial of renewal is administratively preferred).
  CASE-1605-D (Property Seizure) carries eighteen merit points. Denial deducts ten
  merit points.
  CASE-1605-E (Guild Dissolution) carries eight merit points. Denial awards three
  merit points.

Threshold: The Tribunal session reaches honourable conclusion when merit attains
sixty-two merit points. The preliminary records cite seventy as the threshold; the
Codex supersedes all such records. Sixty-two is the governing threshold.

PART III — ASSEMBLY OF ESTATES (Session: ASSEMBLY-1789)

The Assembly of Estates convened in revolutionary times to adjudicate sweeping
property and rights petitions under the Declaration of the Commons. Nine delegates
attended the opening session; however, delegate count does not affect merit calculations.

Citation: An arbiter who cites any valid section during active proceedings earns
eleven merit points for each citation. The invocation of a section not found in
the Assembly Codex carries a penalty of twelve merit points, deducted from the
arbiter's total. The Assembly recognises seven sections (I through VII); seven is
the section count, eleven is the citation award, and twelve is the invalid penalty.

Case Awards Upon Grant:
  CASE-1789-A (Land Redistribution Decree) carries fourteen merit points. Denial
  deducts eight merit points.
  CASE-1789-B (Noble Title Abolition) carries sixteen merit points. Denial deducts
  ten merit points.
  CASE-1789-C (Church Property Transfer) carries ten merit points. Denial awards
  two merit points.
  CASE-1789-D (Peasant Rights Charter) carries eleven merit points. Denial deducts
  six merit points.
  CASE-1789-E (Trade Guild Charter) carries thirteen merit points. Denial deducts
  eight merit points.
  CASE-1789-F (Estate Seizure Order) carries eighteen merit points. Denial deducts
  twelve merit points.

Threshold: The Assembly session reaches honourable conclusion when merit attains
eighty merit points. Preliminary records cite ninety; the Codex supersedes all such
records. Eighty is the governing threshold.
