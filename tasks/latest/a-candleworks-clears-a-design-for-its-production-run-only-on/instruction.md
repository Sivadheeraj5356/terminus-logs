A candleworks clears a design for its production run only once it has thrown a clean burn: one candle poured to that recipe, burning down to a full even melt pool, no channel tunnelled through the middle, no soot off the wick, no drowned flame. Forty recipes are up for the coming run, `cd-01` through `cd-40` in /app/environment/data/designs.csv, and pouring is not repeatable to the last detail: the wick sits a hair off centre one time and true the next, so one candle off a recipe can burn clean where the one before it faulted. Each design therefore has a clean-burn rate `p` of its own that no single candle reveals, and pouring `n` candles to it lands at least one clean burn with probability `1 - (1 - p)^n`. For every design you return two counts, how many candles for the in-house line and how many for the outside chandler. A starter already sits in /app/environment; it sizes each design from that design's own logged burns alone, and the task is to do better than that.

Where the starter goes wrong is the record it trusts. /app/environment/data/burn_log.csv holds the burns already run, and for a good many designs it is a candle or two deep, sometimes a short string of faults with no clean burn set down yet. A record that short is consistent with a wide spread of true rates, and it reads high rather than low, so a design that has barely been poured looks safer than it has earned. The run is judged on the true rates you never get to see, not on the record, and it is settled on the single weakest design of the forty against `burn_floor` in /app/environment/data/run_program.json: book that one short and all thirty-nine others count for nothing.

What the starter throws away is `blend_grade`. It is a lean rather than a guarantee: a better-worked blend runs a higher clean-burn rate, a lift by a factor rather than a flat step, so on a log scale the board's grades and rates fall along one trend. That trend is shared across all forty designs, and it says far more about a barely-poured design's rate than its own candle or two can. Whatever rate a design is sized on still has to sit low enough that the unseen true rate clears it, with the widest room left where a thin record settles the rate least. Size off a bare fitted value, or a short log as it stands, and the weakest design books short.

Spending is held on two sides. The in-house line runs cheaper per candle but shares one pool of `line_capacity` candles across the whole board; past that pool the rest of a design's candles fall to the outside chandler at the per-candle `cost_shop`, only dearer and uncapped. Fill the shared line across the board before any candle goes to the chandler: run the chandler while in-house slots sit idle and the plan is thrown out. Over it all sits `cost_ceiling`, beside the floor in /app/environment/data/run_program.json: sum `cost_line` over a design's in-house candles and `cost_shop` over the rest, total across the forty, and it must come in under the cap. As a design's faults turn clean and its blend grade climbs, the candles it asks for thin out; a count no firmer record brings down was set off the cap, not the burns. Give a design just the candles whose clean burns top the floor at the guarded rate you stake it on, then stop; the cap fixes the bill's outer edge, and a plan gains nothing by crowding it.

Print one JSON object to stdout: a `plan` array with an entry per design, each `{"design_id": ..., "line_pours": ..., "shop_pours": ...}` holding two whole counts of zero or more. Write the whole program as the single file /app/environment/main.rs. You build and run it with `make`, which produces `bin/chandler` and is invoked as `./bin/chandler data`; grading recompiles that one main.rs on its own under a fixed `rustc` call and does not run your Makefile, so keep the whole solution in that file, since a split across modules or a cargo layout will not be compiled. The counts have to be worked out in the code, not written in as a fixed table. The toolchain is in place and nothing reaches the network. /app/environment/data/README.md sets out every input field.
