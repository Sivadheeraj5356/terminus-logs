## Task summary

Chan (chan bi tu) is the hundred-card branch of the Vietnamese To Tom family of card games. Four seats — A, B, C and D — play with a hundred-card deck, and a hand is won the moment a player's nineteen concealed cards together with the winning card settle into ten units: a chan is a pair of identical cards, a ca is two same-rank cards of different suits. The win earns honours, scores points (diem), and — because seats toss cards away as the hand runs on — can draw fines, after which the four seats settle accounts between them. When a hand is won like this the table keeps a terse written account of it: who won and how the winning card reached them, the honours the hand earns under their fixed precedence, the points, any fines the discards trigger, and how the four seats settle up. Play the referee: take one recorded winning declaration and set down exactly that account, working the whole thing out from the recorded hand and the rules of Chan alone — no outside rule service, no stored answers, nothing but the declaration as recorded and the rulebook.
