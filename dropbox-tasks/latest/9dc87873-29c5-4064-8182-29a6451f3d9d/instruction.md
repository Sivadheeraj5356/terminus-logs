The three incident inventories are `/app/task_file/scan_input/credential_incident.txt`, `/app/task_file/scan_input/credential_incident_b.txt`, and `/app/task_file/scan_input/credential_incident_c.txt`. The format is whitespace-separated, ignores blank lines and lines beginning with `#`, and contains:

- `PARAM wave_count W`, `PARAM max_actions_per_wave A`, `PARAM max_software_rotations S`, `PARAM max_dual_trust_mb M`, and `PARAM max_escrow_mb E`. All are positive integers except `S`, which may be zero.
- `WAVE index change_minutes audit_minutes compromised_exposure_multiplier pending_attestation_multiplier`, with one row for each 1-based index from 1 through `W`, in order.
- `SERVICE service_id owner failure_domain hsm_rotation_minutes software_rotation_minutes attestation_minutes compromised_exposure_weight hsm_pending_weight software_pending_weight hsm_escrow_mb software_escrow_mb`. Identifiers are nonempty ASCII tokens, service IDs are unique, and numeric fields are nonnegative integers.
- `MTLS consumer_service_id provider_service_id`. Each pair is unique and names two services.
- `DUAL_TRUST provider_service_id enable_minutes disable_minutes memory_mb exposure_units`. A provider appears in at most one such row.

Each supplied incident has at most 12 waves, four actions per wave, 12 services, 24 `MTLS` rows, and eight `DUAL_TRUST` rows. All calculations fit signed 64-bit integers, each mTLS dependency graph is acyclic, and every inventory has at least one feasible schedule.

Initially every service uses credentials signed by the compromised CA, no rotated service is attested, and no dual-trust window is active. A schedule has exactly `W` waves. Each wave contains at most `A` distinct actions chosen from `ROTATE_HSM_<service_id>`, `ROTATE_SOFTWARE_<service_id>`, `ATTEST_<service_id>`, `DUAL_TRUST_ON_<provider_service_id>`, and `DUAL_TRUST_OFF_<provider_service_id>`. Every service must use exactly one rotation mode and then be attested exactly once, with at most `S` services using software rotation. HSM and software rotations use their corresponding rotation minutes, pending-attestation weight, and escrow storage from the `SERVICE` row. Attestation is allowed only when the service was already rotated at the start of the wave, so rotation and attestation of one service cannot share a wave. A dual-trust window may be enabled at most once, may be retired only while active, cannot be re-enabled, and must be retired by the end.

Actions in a wave are simultaneous and all remaining rules are checked on the end-of-wave state. Rotation and dual-trust actions consume that wave's `change_minutes`; attestation consumes its `audit_minutes`. Neither capacity may be exceeded. At most one rotation or attestation action may touch a given `failure_domain` in one wave. Active dual-trust memory is the sum of `memory_mb` for active windows and cannot exceed `M`. Escrow storage is the sum of the chosen mode's escrow value for services that are rotated but not yet attested and cannot exceed `E`.

For every `MTLS consumer provider`, if the two services use different credential generations after a wave, the provider's dual-trust window must be active after that wave. A service may be attested only when all its direct providers are attested by the end of that wave; same-wave provider and consumer attestations satisfy this rule. A dual-trust window may be enabled in the wave that creates a credential-generation mismatch and retired in the wave that removes the last mismatch. `DUAL_TRUST_ON` and `DUAL_TRUST_OFF` for the same provider cannot share a wave. Empty waves are valid.

After each wave, add `compromised_exposure_multiplier * compromised_exposure_weight` for every service still using compromised credentials, `pending_attestation_multiplier` times the chosen mode's pending-attestation weight for every rotated but unattested service, and `exposure_units` for every active dual-trust window. Their sum over all waves is `credential_exposure_units`. Choose the feasible schedule with the lowest `credential_exposure_units`, then the lowest peak active dual-trust memory, then the lowest peak escrow storage, then the lowest total minutes consumed by all actions. If still tied, compare wave 1's sorted action ID array lexicographically, then wave 2's, and so on; strings compare by unsigned ASCII bytes and a proper array prefix is smaller.
