I'm working out how far a surface-catalysed batch reaction actually gets, run by run, from time-lapse counts under the microscope. In each run a large batch of tagged reactant molecules starts adsorbed on the surface at time 0, and from there a molecule can convert to the product I care about, hit an irreversible side-reaction and decompose, or desorb and drift out of the field of view we can watch — and there's one more wrinkle in between: partway along a molecule can pass through a detectable activated intermediate, after which it both converts faster and desorbs faster, because the activated form is more reactive but more weakly held. That activation isn't a one-way step, though — the activated form is only loosely committed, so a fair fraction of it just relaxes back to the plain adsorbed state before doing anything else, and the two on-surface states keep trading population back and forth the whole time rather than draining in a single cascade. The product itself is not stuck in the patch either: once formed it can desorb and leave the field of view, so the product count we see is only the product that is still on the surface at that instant, not the cumulative number of molecules that have converted. What I need for each run is the cumulative conversion — the fraction of the whole starting batch that has become product by time t = 5 in the run's own time units. The catch is that the molecules that desorb don't stop reacting, they just leave the patch we can see, so they still go on to convert or decompose off-camera; and because the activated ones desorb fastest, the molecules that vanish from view are exactly the ones most likely to be about to convert, so anything read straight off the counts comes out too low. Desorbed product has already converted, so it still counts toward that cumulative conversion even though it has left the image.

Each run sits in its own folder under `/app/reactor` (run1, run2, and so on), and every folder has a `counts.csv`: one row per sampling time, with columns `time_s` (the time in the run's units), `n_adsorbed`, `n_activated`, `n_product` and `n_decomposed` — how many of the tagged molecules were seen on the surface in each of those four states at that instant. The first row is time 0, where every molecule is adsorbed and none has reacted yet, so that count is the size of the starting batch. Desorbed molecules (reactant or product) simply drop out of all four columns from the moment they leave the field of view, and the counts carry the usual bit of measurement scatter.

For each run, work out the true cumulative conversion at t = 5 for the whole starting batch — including the molecules that desorbed as reactant and finished converting out of view, and including the product that has already desorbed — and write it to `conversion.txt` in that run's own folder, one line only, as a number with four digits after the decimal point (example shape `0.1234`). Write the solution in C, and leave every `counts.csv` exactly as you found it; scratch files elsewhere under `/app/reactor` are fine.
