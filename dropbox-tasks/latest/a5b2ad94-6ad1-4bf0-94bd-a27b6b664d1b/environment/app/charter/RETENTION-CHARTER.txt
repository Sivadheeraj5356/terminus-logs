SPOOLWARD RETENTION CHARTER
Colverton retention plane operations bulletin. The clauses below are numbered
L1 onward. They are the whole charter; nothing outside them binds. Where
two clauses bear on one matter they agree.

L1. The warden spoolward administers the plane's log retention:
it works each cycle's change orders against the plane register, the source
inventory, the operator roster and the standing streams, closes every cycle with
hold expiry and an overflow purge, and seals how the plane stands as one
report.
L2. A cycle is a positive integer index. The cycles directory fixes the
cycles of the term: its files taken in ascending name order are cycle 1,
cycle 2, and so on, and their count is cycles_run.
L3. All counts, volumes and budgets in the report are integers. No
fractional value ever appears in the report.
L4. The plane root holds plane.ini, sources.tsv, operators.tsv, an indices
directory and a cycles directory.
L5. Every file of the root is UTF-8 text, and the warden leaves every file
under the root byte for byte as it found it.

THE PLANE REGISTER

L6. plane.ini is read line by line; blank lines and lines opening with a
number sign carry nothing; a line [name] opens the section so named; a
line key = value binds key to value in the open section, blanks trimmed
from both ends of each.
L7. Section [plane] declares the integers plane_revision, index_budget and
hold_term.
L8. Section [collectors] declares one binding per ingest collector; the collector is the
key and the value is the single mark -.
L9. Section [indices] declares one binding per log index; the key is
the index and the value is the collector it hangs from. An index hangs from
exactly one collector for the whole term.
L10. The indices stand in registration order: the order of their bindings
in [indices].
L11. For each declared index the roster indices/<index>.tsv carries
one channel per line: channel and note, split by a single tab. The note
column binds nothing.
L12. A channel belongs to exactly one index, its roster's index, for
the whole term. A channel never named by any roster is unknown to the plane.
L13. sources.tsv carries one source per line: source, volume, class and note,
split by single tabs. The volume is a non-negative integer of gigabytes; the class
is retained or rotating; the note column binds nothing.
L14. A source carries its declared gigabytes whenever it stands on a channel, on
whatever index; a source's volume and class never change through the term.
L15. operators.tsv carries one operator per line: name, role, holdings and
badge, split by single tabs. The role is curator or operator. For a
curator the holdings field lists opened indices; for an operator it
lists charged collectors; either list is semicolon-split, or the single mark -
for none. The badge column binds nothing.
L16. streams.ndjson carries the streams standing when the term opens, one JSON
object per line, with members channel, source, opened (an integer cycle)
and at times pinned.
L17. An opening stream binds its source to its channel from cycle opened and
opens pinned when pinned is true. Opening streams stand in file order, all of
them ahead of every stream made during the term.
L18. Each cycles file carries one change order per line, a single JSON
object; blank lines carry nothing and are no orders.

STREAMS

L19. A stream binds one source to one channel. While it stands, its channel is
taken and its source is bound, and it carries its source's gigabytes upon its
channel's index.
L20. A source carries at most one standing stream across the whole plane, not
per index; a second stream for a bound source is refused wherever its
channel lies.
L21. A channel carries at most one standing stream.
L22. A stream's class is the class of its source under L13, retained or
rotating, and never changes.
L23. A stream's opened cycle is the cycle of its making and never changes;
an opening stream is opened the cycle its streams.ndjson record names.
L24. Stream age runs by opened cycle first and standing order second; the
younger stream has the greater opened cycle, and of equal opening the later
standing order under L17.
L25. At any instant a stream record stands in exactly one of LIVE, DETACHED,
PURGED, FLUSHED or DECOMMISSIONED; the report tells a live stream on a quiesced
index apart as DORMANT under L120.
L26. DETACHED, PURGED, FLUSHED and DECOMMISSIONED are final for the record; the
record keeps that state to the term's end.
L27. A detached or purged channel is free again under L113 and may take a fresh
stream; the fresh stream is a new record with its own opened cycle.
L28. A flush bars its source-channel pair: no stream of that same pair is
applied again in the term, though the channel itself frees and either party
may pair elsewhere.
L29. A decommissioned index's channels are dead: nothing is routed upon them
for the rest of the term.

OPERATORS AND AUTHORITY

L30. Connection work is ROUTE, DETACH, HOLD, RELEASE, PIN and UNPIN; it belongs
to curators alone. Collector work is QUIESCE, RESUME, DECOMMISSION, RETUNE and
FLUSH; it belongs to operators alone.
L31. A curator's authority over connection work runs through the
governing index: for a ROUTE, DETACH, PIN or UNPIN, the index of the named
channel; for a HOLD or RELEASE, the index fixed by L66. The work is
authorized when the curator's holdings carry that index.
L32. An operator's authority over collector work runs through the governing
collector: for a FLUSH, the collector of the named channel's index; for a QUIESCE,
RESUME or RETUNE, the collector of the named index; for a DECOMMISSION, the named
collector itself. The work is authorized when the operator's holdings carry that
collector.
L33. A change order whose kind and members are sound under L37 but whose
actor bears the wrong role for the kind, or bears the right role without the
holding L31 or L32 demands, is answered UNAUTHORIZED and changes nothing.

ORDER AND SCREENING

L34. Within a cycle the change orders are answered strictly in the order
their lines stand in the file; nothing is answered across cycles.
L35. Every sound order is answered with exactly one of APPLIED, DENIED, NOOP
or UNAUTHORIZED and enters the journal with that answer; a screened line is
answered VOID, is counted in orders_seen and by_disposition, and enters no
journal.
L36. Screening is judged before authority, and authority under L33 before
any law of state; an order failing an earlier judgment never reaches a later
one.
L37. A line is VOID when it is not one well-formed JSON object; or it lacks
seq (an integer), kind (one of the eleven kinds of L39) or actor (a name in
operators.tsv); or it lacks a member its kind demands under L40 to L45, or
carries one of the wrong type; or it names a channel, source, index or
collector the plane does not know; or it is a RETUNE whose budget member is not an
integer of zero or above; or it is a HOLD or RELEASE naming both a source and
a channel, or neither.
L38. Within one cycle the seq of each sound order must exceed the seq of the
last sound order before it, and the first sound order's seq must exceed
zero. A violating line is VOID, and a VOID line moves no seq.
L39. The eleven kinds are ROUTE, DETACH, HOLD, RELEASE, PIN, UNPIN, QUIESCE,
RESUME, DECOMMISSION, RETUNE and FLUSH.
L40. ROUTE demands channel and source, strings naming a rostered channel and an
inventoried source.
L41. DETACH, PIN, UNPIN and FLUSH demand channel, a string naming a rostered
channel.
L42. HOLD and RELEASE demand exactly one of source or channel, a string naming
an inventoried source or a rostered channel.
L43. QUIESCE, RESUME and RETUNE demand index, a string naming a
declared index; RETUNE further demands budget, an integer of zero or
above.
L44. DECOMMISSION demands collector, a string naming a declared collector.
L45. Members beyond those a kind demands bind nothing and screen nothing.

THE JOURNAL SUBJECT

L46. Each journal row carries cycle, seq, kind, subject and disposition, and
nothing else.
L47. The subject of ROUTE, DETACH, PIN, UNPIN and FLUSH is the channel; of a
source-named HOLD or RELEASE the source; of a channel-named HOLD or RELEASE the
channel; of QUIESCE, RESUME and RETUNE the index; of DECOMMISSION the collector.
L48. The journal holds every sound order of the term in answered order, cycle
first and line order within it.

ROUTE

L49. A ROUTE naming a channel and source that are already one standing stream
is NOOP.
L50. A ROUTE onto an index not SERVING is DENIED.
L51. A ROUTE naming a taken channel is DENIED.
L52. A ROUTE naming a bound source is DENIED, wherever in the plane the
source's standing stream lies.
L53. A ROUTE naming a pair barred under L28 is DENIED.
L54. A ROUTE clearing L49 to L53 is APPLIED: a new stream stands, opened the
cycle in hand, classed under L22, unpinned and unheld.
L55. A ROUTE is weighed for overflow nowhere at its instant; the index
answers overflow only at the close under L95, so an APPLIED ROUTE may still be
purged at the close of its own cycle or a later cycle.

DETACH AND FLUSH

L56. A DETACH naming a channel with nothing standing is NOOP.
L57. A DETACH of a held stream is DENIED; the hold binds the detach whichever
side of L64 declared it.
L58. A DETACH clearing L56 and L57 is APPLIED: the stream goes DETACHED, its pin
is void, and its channel frees at once.
L59. A FLUSH naming a channel with nothing standing is NOOP.
L60. A FLUSH of a standing stream is APPLIED whether or not the stream is held
or pinned; a hold binds the detach and the overflow purge, never the operator's
force flush.
L61. A flushed stream goes FLUSHED, its pin and any hold on it are void, its
channel frees at once, and its pair is barred under L28.

HOLDS

L62. A hold is a retention hold placed on a standing stream; it guards the stream
against DETACH under L57 and against the overflow purge under L92.
L63. A HOLD or RELEASE reaches its stream through either side: the named
channel's standing stream, or the named source's standing stream. Either naming
binds the one stream.
L64. A HOLD or RELEASE over a channel or source with nothing standing is NOOP,
whoever brings it; its authority is not examined further.
L65. The governing index of a HOLD or RELEASE, for L31, is the index of
the stream's channel as reached under L63.
L66. A HOLD on a stream already under an unexpired hold is NOOP.
L67. A HOLD clearing L64 and L66 is APPLIED: the hold stands from the cycle
in hand.
L68. A hold endures hold_term cycles: declared in cycle d, it stands through
cycle d + hold_term - 1 and expires at that cycle's close, in the close
ladder of L87 before any overflow purge.
L69. A RELEASE of an unexpired hold is APPLIED and lifts it at once; a RELEASE
with no unexpired hold to lift is NOOP.
L70. A hold is void with its stream: whatever ends, flushes or decommissions the stream
ends the hold.

PINS

L71. A PIN names a channel; with nothing standing there it is NOOP; on a
stream already pinned it is NOOP; otherwise it is APPLIED and the stream is
pinned.
L72. An UNPIN names a channel; it is APPLIED when the standing stream is pinned
and NOOP otherwise.
L73. A pin guards its stream against the overflow purge alone: it does not bind
DETACH, does not bind FLUSH, stands through quiescence and resumption, and is
void under decommission and under every end of its stream.

QUIESCE AND RESUME

L74. An index stands SERVING, QUIESCED or DECOMMISSIONED; every index opens the
term SERVING.
L75. A QUIESCE of a SERVING index is APPLIED: the index goes QUIESCED.
Of an index already QUIESCED, NOOP. Of a DECOMMISSIONED index, DENIED.
L76. A quiesced index takes no new stream under L50, and its channels are
not free under L113; its standing streams stand, their pins and holds stand,
and the overflow purge passes the index over entirely.
L77. A RESUME of an QUIESCED index is APPLIED and returns it to SERVING
at once; of a SERVING index, NOOP; of a DECOMMISSIONED index, DENIED.
L78. Quiescence leaves no residue: a resumed index carries stream exactly
as it would had the quiescence never stood, save what the calendar did in
between; in particular a resumed index is weighed for overflow at the
close of the cycle it returns.

DECOMMISSION

L79. A DECOMMISSION names a collector. Of a collector already decommissioned, NOOP; otherwise
APPLIED.
L80. An applied DECOMMISSION puts the collector beyond use for the term: every index
of the collector goes DECOMMISSIONED from whatever standing it held, every standing
stream in those indices goes DECOMMISSIONED in the same instant, every such pin
and hold is void, and every channel of those indices is dead under L29.
L81. Decommission is terminal for collector, indices, and records alike; no
clause returns them.
L82. A QUIESCE, RESUME or RETUNE naming an index of a decommissioned collector is
DENIED under L75, L77 and L84.
L83. Dormant records are beyond decommission's reach: a detached, purge or
flushed record keeps its state when its index is decommissioned.

RETUNE

L84. A RETUNE names an index and an integer budget. Of a decommissioned index,
DENIED. Naming the budget the index already carries in effect, NOOP.
Otherwise APPLIED: the index's budget is the named value from that instant.
L85. An index's effective budget is its own applied binding when one
stands, and the plane's index_budget when none does.
L86. A budget binds only the overflow purge under L91; it changes no standing
stream at its instant.

THE CLOSE

L87. Each cycle closes after its last line is answered. The close is a
ladder of two rungs, always in this order: hold expiry, then the overflow
purge.
L88. Hold expiry ends every hold whose final cycle under L68 is the closing
cycle.
L89. The overflow purge visits each index once, in registration order under
L10, passing over QUIESCED and DECOMMISSIONED indices.
L90. An index's volume is the sum of the source volumes of its standing streams;
a stream's volume is its source's gigabytes under L14.
L91. At each index the purge weighs the index's volume against the index's
effective budget under L85; the index is in overflow while its volume stands
strictly above its budget.
L92. While an index is in overflow, the purge drops one purgeable stream and
weighs again; with none purgeable the index is left in overflow.
L93. Purgeable at an index is every standing stream of that index's channels
that is rotating, unpinned, and under no unexpired hold.
L94. Of the purgeable streams the purge drops the one of greatest volume; of
equal volume it drops the younger under L24, that is the greater opened cycle
and then the later standing order.
L95. A purged stream goes PURGED and its channel frees at once; retained streams, pinned
streams and held streams are never purged.
L96. The overflow purge answers no journal row; its work shows in by_state, in
by_index and nowhere in the journal.

THE REPORT

L97. The report is one JSON object of exactly nine members: plane_revision,
cycles_run, orders_seen, by_disposition, by_state, by_index, journal,
data_digest and report_digest.
L98. plane_revision carries the [plane] binding. cycles_run counts under L2.
orders_seen counts every order line of the term, sound and screened alike.
L99. by_disposition tallies the five answers of L35 over every line of the
term; all five keys stand whatever their counts.
L100. by_state tallies the stream records of the term, opening and made alike,
under their L25 states with DORMANT told apart under L120; all six keys stand
whatever their counts.
L101. by_index carries one entry per declared index, keyed by index
name, each entry exactly standing, live and volume: standing is the index's
L74 standing as the term ends; live counts the index's standing streams,
zero for a DECOMMISSIONED index; volume sums the source volumes of those standing
streams under L90, zero for a DECOMMISSIONED index.
L102. A live stream on a quiesced index is a standing stream: it is counted in
that index's live and volume under L101 though it is told apart as DORMANT in
by_state.
L103. The journal stands under L46 to L48.
L104. The report is serialized canonically: object members ascending by key
at every depth, no spaces about any token, ASCII strings, bare integers. The
written file is this canonical serialization followed by a single line feed
and nothing else around it; that closing line feed frames the file alone and
is no part of the serialized bytes the report seal of L123 weighs.
L105. The warden prints nothing to standard output or standard error on a
sound run.
L106. Before the report is written, every entry in the report's directory,
files and directories alike, is cleared away, and the directory then holds
the report alone; a missing directory is created.
L107. Run twice over an unchanged root, the warden writes the same report
byte for byte.
L108. The single argument --selfcheck ends a run at once: nothing is read of
the root, nothing is written anywhere, and the exit is zero.
L109. A sound run exits zero.

STANDINGS AND VOLUME

L110. A channel is taken while a standing stream binds it.
L111. A source is bound while a standing stream binds it.
L112. A standing stream is a record in LIVE, on an index of any standing;
display under L120 never changes what stands.
L113. A free channel is a rostered channel of a SERVING index with no
standing stream upon it.
L114. The close ladder of L87 belongs to every cycle, the last cycle
included; the report reads the plane after the final close.
L115. The term's records are never discarded: every stream record of the term
appears in exactly one by_state tally.
L116. Registration order serves the overflow purge alone; by_index is keyed
ascending by name and the journal stands in answered order.
L117. The eleven kinds are closed: a line whose kind is any other string is
screened under L37, and no kind beyond the eleven ever reaches the journal.
L118. The five dispositions and the six stream states are closed enums; the
report never carries a key beyond them in by_disposition or by_state.
L119. orders_seen equals the sum of the five by_disposition counts, and the
journal's length equals orders_seen less the VOID count; a VOID line is
counted but takes no journal row.

DISPLAY

L120. The by_state tally and no other clause tells streams apart by quiescence:
a LIVE stream whose index stands QUIESCED as the term ends is tallied DORMANT;
every other LIVE stream is tallied LIVE. A DORMANT stream remains a LIVE record for
every clause save this tally.

THE TWO SEALS

L121. data_digest is the first sixteen hex digits of the SHA-256 taken over
the root's files: every file under the root, walked ascending by full
relative path with forward slashes, and for each file the path's UTF-8
bytes, one NUL byte, the file's bytes, one NUL byte.
L122. As a toy: a root holding one file a.txt of the two bytes x, LF digests
the byte run a.txt NUL x LF NUL.
L123. report_digest is the first sixteen hex digits of the SHA-256 taken over
the report serialized as in L104 with the report_digest member wholly absent
and without the framing line feed of L104; it is reckoned after data_digest
is in place. The bytes hashed end at the report's closing brace: the trailing
line feed of the written file is never among them.
L124. The two seals are lowercase hex, sixteen digits each.
L125. The data seal weighs every file under the root, named by this charter
or not, and nothing else: not the report, not directory names, not any file
beyond the root.
L126. The warden reads the plane root named by the SPOOLWARD_PLANE environment
variable, and /app/plane when that variable is unset or empty; it writes the
report at the full file path named by the SPOOLWARD_REPORT environment
variable, and /app/output/spoolward_report.json when that variable is unset or
empty.

FURTHER LAW

L127. The journal rows carry their five members in canonical member order like
every object of the report.
L128. by_index entries carry exactly standing, live and volume, and never a
fourth member.
L129. The warden makes no change of its own: no order, no clause and no purge
ever alters plane.ini, the rosters, the inventory, streams.ndjson or any cycles
file.
L130. An UNPIN, RELEASE or RESUME never fails for lateness: where its object
is already absent the answer is NOOP, never DENIED, save the decommissioned-index
law of L77.
L131. Where an order is DENIED or NOOP the plane stands exactly as before the
order was read.
L132. An UNAUTHORIZED order changes nothing, whatever its kind demanded.
L133. Holds do not stack: at most one unexpired hold stands on a stream, and a
second declaration is NOOP under L66 whichever side names it.
L134. A hold declared in cycle d under hold_term 1 stands through cycle d
alone and expires at that cycle's close.
L135. The pair bar of L28 is keyed by source and channel together: the same
source may take another channel, and the same channel another source, unbarred.
L136. Nothing restores a barred pair: no detach, no resume, no retune and no
release touches L28.
L137. An index whose effective budget is not less than its volume is not
in overflow; the purge leaves it untouched however its streams stand.
L138. The overflow purge and the close ladder do not reorder the journal:
dropped lines aside, it is the term's change orders as answered.
