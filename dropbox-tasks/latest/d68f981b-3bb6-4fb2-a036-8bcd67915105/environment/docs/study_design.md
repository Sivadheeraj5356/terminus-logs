# Study design

## Setting

A large employer offered a voluntary wellbeing program to its staff over a
single year. Some employees enrolled and some did not, and for every employee we
observe a wellbeing index recorded at the end of the year. The goal is the effect
of enrolling in the program on that end-of-year index.

## How enrollment happened

Enrollment was not assigned at random. Employees chose whether to join, and the
program also encouraged some employees more than others. Two kinds of forces
shaped who ended up enrolled.

The first is a set of pre-enrollment characteristics recorded for every employee
at the start of the year: eight baseline indices describing health and lifestyle,
and the number of years the employee has been with the company. Several of these
characteristics also bear on how an employee's wellbeing evolves over the year,
so employees who enrolled and employees who did not already differed on them
before the program began.

Beyond any single measure acting on its own, self-selection into the program was
also governed by combinations of the baseline measures acting together. Each such
combined influence involves a few of the baseline measures, with each measure
entering through a periodic transform that rises and falls repeatedly across that
measure's range rather than trending steadily in one direction, and the per-measure
periodic terms enter multiplicatively rather than additively. Because they combine
as a product, a combined influence is sizable only when every measure in its group
happens to sit at the right part of its own cycle at once, and averages to nothing
otherwise. That multiplicative, oscillating shape means the influence leaves no
footprint in any single baseline measure on its own or in any pair, and no
polynomial in the measures reproduces it; it is present only when the whole group
of measures is considered together, with each measure's periodic term repeating at
a rate characteristic to that influence. Enrollment was shaped by more than one such
combined influence, each involving a different small group of the baseline measures,
and the same combined influences also bear on end-of-year wellbeing.

## The outreach count

`outreach_contacts` is the number of reminder calls and emails the program sent
to an employee during the enrollment window. It is a record of how hard the
program worked to sign a given employee up, generated by the program's own
targeting, and it strongly predicts whether the employee enrolled. It is not a
health or lifestyle attribute of the employee and carries no direct effect on the
end-of-year wellbeing index. The program aimed its outreach using the employee's
baseline profile, so the count is correlated with the same baseline
characteristics that differ between employees who enrolled and employees who did
not.

## The effect being estimated

The program does not change every enrollee's wellbeing index by the same amount;
the benefit is larger for some employees than for others, and how large it is
depends on how long the employee has been with the company. The quantity of
interest is the average effect across the entire population of employees
represented in these records, that is, the average end-of-year wellbeing change
that enrolling causes for an employee drawn at random from this population,
separating the program's own effect from the pre-existing differences that shaped
who enrolled.

## Outcome

`wellbeing_index` is a composite wellbeing score, measured once, at the end of the
year, for every employee.
