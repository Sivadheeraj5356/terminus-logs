Difficulty

The batch reads like a heap of tokens, so the first wrong turn is to xor the rod lengths as plain
nim. The heavy blade eats a unit as sawdust, so the two rods left add to one less than the rod
sawn, which pulls a rod's worth off its length and leaves a lone rod of two a loss with nothing to
saw. The harder trap is that the gaps in the row set the blade: an odd count of empty rods runs it
true and eats nothing, so that same rod of two now saws into two ones and wins. A reader who fixes
one blade for every row calls every gap-carrying batch backwards.

Solution

Work up two worth tables from the short rods first, one for the heavy blade where the two rods add
to one less than the rod and one for the true blade where they add to the whole rod. Count the
gaps in the row to pick the blade, read each rod's worth off that table, xor them for the verdict,
and on a win search the rods for a cut that leaves the combined worth nought.

Verification

The worth reading is cross-checked against a full game search that knows no worth, over every
small batch under each parity of gaps, which pins the winning rule and the blade the gaps set. The
tool then runs over many random and adversarial batches, each verdict matched to the sweep and
each reported cut replayed under the blade in force to confirm it lands a loss. Hand cases pin the
two beside a gap, the echo and numbering, the out-of-range and junk lines, the mill file and the
exit codes.
