A transit authority runs several bus lines that all share one common headway: every line repeats on the same fixed cycle length, and the only thing left to decide for each line is its departure offset within that cycle. Every line calls at one interchange, landing at a platform of the concourse partway through its run and pulling out again from another platform a little later, both at times fixed by the line's offset plus its known running times. Each row of the transfer list is a body of passengers off one line who want another. They have to cross the concourse on foot, and that walk eats into the time they have to make the connection; the interchange's passage map gives the time of each passage running directly between a pair of platforms, in a building that grew piecemeal and confines nobody to a single passage. A connection is comfortable when the connecting bus pulls out a short, comfortable interval after its passengers reach its platform; because the timetable is cyclic, that interval is measured around the cycle, so a connection can be good even when the departure clock time is earlier than the arrival.

Rows are grouped into passenger flows. A flow appearing in more than one row is one body of people offered a choice of connecting lines: they travel together and ride one bus. Nobody is dispatching them, and they get on the first bus they can catch: of the connections open to them they ride one that pulls out as soon as any of them does. The authority cannot wave them onto a later bus to keep an earlier one free, and cannot hold them back while a heavier crowd goes first, so who rides what follows from the offsets rather than from anything the authority picks. A bus is not infinitely large, and everybody who boards a line's departure has to be carried away on that one bus, within its seats. Some lines also share a physical departure terminus, and any two lines sharing one must keep their offsets apart by a required minimum spacing, again measured around the cycle; this spacing is a hard requirement, independent of the passenger weights.

Write a MiniZinc model at /app/model.mzn that reads the instance in /app/instance.dzn and prints both the chosen offset of every line and who boards what. A starter model is already present; make it satisfy the full specification and maximise the total weight of the passenger flows that are actually carried away. The full problem statement is in /app/docs/problem.md, the parameters in /app/docs/data_dictionary.md, and the output lines in /app/docs/output_format.md. Run your model any time with /app/run_model.sh.
