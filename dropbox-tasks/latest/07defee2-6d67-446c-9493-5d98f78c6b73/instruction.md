Chan (chan bi tu) is the hundred-card branch of the Vietnamese To Tom family of card games. Four seats — A, B, C and D — play with a hundred-card deck, and a hand is won the moment a player's nineteen concealed cards together with the winning card settle into ten units: a chan is a pair of identical cards, a ca is two same-rank cards of different suits. The win earns honours, scores points (diem), and — because seats toss cards away as the hand runs on — can draw fines, after which the four seats settle accounts between them.

When a hand is won like this the table keeps a terse written account of it: who won and how the winning card reached them, the honours the hand earns under their fixed precedence, the points, any fines the discards trigger, and how the four seats settle up. Play the referee: take one recorded winning declaration and set down exactly that account, working the whole thing out from the recorded hand and the rules of Chan alone — no outside rule service, no stored answers, nothing but the declaration as recorded and the rulebook.

The honours stack and exclude one another under a fixed precedence, and the discard log triggers a separate family of fines, so both dimensions have to be applied densely and exactly for the account to come out right, down to its closing newline. The whole of Chan — the deck and which cards count as red, the chan and ca units, every honour with its precedence and points, the fine triggers, the settlement arithmetic, the fault order and its line numbers, every token the account can print, the canonical ordering of the layout, and that closing newline — is laid out in the rulebook at /app/docs/rules.md; follow it to the letter. As a small boundary case, a record that is only the line `stop` yields `Fault = line:2 reason:malformed` then `Result = void`, each closed by a newline.
