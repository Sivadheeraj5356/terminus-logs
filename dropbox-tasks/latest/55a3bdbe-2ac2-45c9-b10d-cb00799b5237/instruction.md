A wildlife photographer is planning the next tour and has forty subjects lined up to shoot, su-01 through su-40. Each one needs a frame budget going in: how many frames to spend from a shared public hide, and how many from a private guided sit. The hide is the cheap route. It bills at `hide_cost` per frame but works off a single ration, `hide_frame_budget` frames to be split among all forty subjects, so once that ration is gone the rest of a subject's frames come from the guided route at the steeper `guide_cost`. What the tour is really after is one keeper on every subject. Any single frame either keeps (the subject sharp, an eye lit, wingtips inside the frame) or it is a miss, at a rate that belongs to the subject; because frames fall independently, n of them bring home at least one keeper with probability 1-(1-p)^n. The aim is to carry every subject over the `keeper_floor` published in /app/environment/data/shoot_policy.json at the lowest committed cost the plan can reach.

Committed cost is totted up across the list: each subject's hide frames at its `hide_cost`, plus its guided frames at its `guide_cost`. The `cost_ceiling` in that same policy file is firm. A plan that runs past it is refused outright, and so is one that lets any subject come in under the floor. What the ceiling leaves you is slack to go deep on a handful of subjects, not to raise the whole list a step. A subject whose rate you genuinely cannot fix is worth a heavy count, and the budget will wear that; try instead to edge all forty upward at once and the small overages pile up until the total clears the cap.

The scoring never looks at the log. It works from the keeper rate each subject will post on a fresh tour, a true per-frame rate settled in advance and withheld, and it rebuilds 1-(1-p)^n on that withheld rate, subject by subject. The floor binds jointly, and only ever bites in one place: a plan stands or falls on whichever subject the live rates leave weakest, so a fat margin on the strong subjects does nothing for one that lands short.

Which is where /app/environment/data/frame_log.csv turns treacherous. A subject you have shot only a handful of times has barely declared itself. One or two frames that happened to keep can dress a poor subject up as a safe one, while a jumpy subject that has burned through frame after frame with nothing worth saving is not being coy, its rate really is that low. A rate the record cannot settle should be read toward the low end of where it might sit, and the subjects the record leaves most in doubt are the ones to bank deepest. Size a full, steady record to about what it asks for. Size a thin or empty one as though the truth could be poorer than it looks.

Write the framer in Rust under /app/environment. `make` builds `bin/framer`, and `./bin/framer data` reads /app/environment/data and writes the plan to stdout: one JSON object holding a `plan` array, an entry per subject reading `{"subject_id": ..., "hide_frames": ..., "guided_frames": ...}`, every count a whole number of zero or more. Grading recompiles the source and runs it again, so the counts have to be produced by the program, not pasted in. The Rust toolchain is already installed, the machine has no network, and /app/environment/data/README.md describes the input files.
